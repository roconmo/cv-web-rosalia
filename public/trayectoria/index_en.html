About me

I come from technology and now work on processes

I have over 20 years in complex technology environments: data analysis, cloud, transformation projects. Over those years I saw the same thing many times: information doesn't reach the people who need it, and work gets stuck at the same points.

Now I help companies see where they lose time and what to change first. I speak the language of business and the language of technology.

I am currently working on a process diagnosis for a family-owned construction materials distribution company, focusing on cross-department coordination and improvement proposals for management.

  • Diagnosis based on direct observation, interviews and process analysis
  • Project management and change implementation support
  • Master in Project Management · IEBS Business School
  • Open to consulting projects or joining a team in-house

What I bring

I work with executives who know something isn't working well internally, but aren't sure exactly where or how to tackle it.

Organisational diagnosis

I analyse how work flows between departments to identify bottlenecks, critical dependencies and improvement opportunities that aren't always visible from the inside.

Direct observation, team interviews, process mapping and friction point analysis.

Process improvement

I design initiatives aimed at simplifying processes, improving cross-team coordination and making day-to-day execution easier.

Concrete, prioritised proposals adapted to what the team can implement.

Project management

I turn the diagnosis into an action plan with clear phases, owners and metrics, and support implementation so that changes stick.

When the project involves technology, I act as a bridge between technical and business teams so that business and technical teams understand the same thing.

What you get

At the end of the diagnosis you get:

  • a clear picture of how work flows;
  • the main bottlenecks identified;
  • initiatives prioritised by impact and effort;
  • a roadmap for implementing improvements.
